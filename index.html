Motivation

Successful demonstrations contain many ways to move.
We want to choose the quality, and improve beyond what was demonstrated.

RQ1

Can one policy offer control over execution quality?

A task can be completed in many ways, with different levels of speed, smoothness, and safety. How can we learn from this diversity to let users choose the quality they want?

RQ2

Demonstrations cover a limited range.

Selecting a behavior within the initial data is only the first step. Can a controllable policy also learn to reach higher quality beyond those demonstrations?

Method

1. Condition on quality.

Measure each successful trajectory and quantize its score into equal-frequency bins. Train the policy on all successful trajectories, conditioned on their quality labels. At inference time, request a label to select the execution behavior.

Loading method diagram…

2. Expand the controllable range.

Collect rollouts with the highest label and keep successes. Measure their achieved quality, recompute labels for the entire accumulated dataset, and retrain. Repeat as better experience enters the data.

Result

Better quality. Higher success.

QCP improves execution quality and task success together over iterations. It learns from all successful rollouts, using quality labels to select better behavior.

Beyond the initial best.

New rollouts shift the quality distribution beyond the best initial demonstrations. Repeated collection and relabeling expand the range of behaviors the policy can achieve.

Improve multiple qualities together.

One policy is conditioned on six quality attributes at once. Across four tasks, QCP improves most attributes over both initial BC and BC trained on accumulated rollouts.
